# Nexus-Sync, product context

## Purpose

Nexus-Sync gives every developer an ephemeral cloud development environment that mirrors the laptop in real time. Engineers write code locally in their familiar editor; Nexus-Sync streams it into an isolated cloud pod with hot reload, anonymized production data, instant shareable preview URLs, and multiplayer collaboration on the same running application.

The wedge: stop spending 30 percent of engineering time on local Docker setup, "works on my machine" tickets, and stale staging environments.

## Users

**Primary.** Platform and DevOps engineers at enterprise scale, organizations with 200 or more engineers. They evaluate developer experience tooling on behalf of dozens to hundreds of internal users. They are technically deep, skeptical of marketing, allergic to hype, and judge a tool by the quality of its docs and how its claims hold up under inspection.

**Secondary.** Staff and senior engineers at growth stage startups (Series B through D) who feel the slow inner loop daily and have informal authority to introduce new tooling.

**Not the audience.** Weekend hobbyists, no code users, students. The page does not coddle.

## Register

Brand. The landing page is the product's first impression. Design carries weight equal to the words.

## Brand voice

Two lanes, blended.

1. Technical precise, like the Linear changelog or the Vercel CLI reference. Specific numbers. Named primitives. No "blazing fast." Every claim is verifiable or it is cut.
2. Editorial confident, like a long form Stripe post or a Bret Victor essay. Calm. Long form patience. Willing to spend a paragraph on a single idea instead of a bullet list.

The blend: write the technical claim first, then earn it with one editorial sentence.

## Strategic principle

Craft is the proof. A platform engineer landing here should think "the team that built this site will not waste my time." Every element of the page is part of the demo. If the page itself feels rushed, the product feels rushed.

## Anti references

The developer tool category has converged on a single visual language. Nexus-Sync rejects it.

- Dark mode plus cyan, magenta, electric blue neon as the default palette. Vercel, Linear, Supabase, Railway, Cursor, Modal, Neon all live there.
- Isometric 3D illustrations of laptops, Kubernetes hexagons, glowing data bridges, floating server pods.
- Animated gradient borders, glassmorphic cards stacked four deep, shimmer backgrounds.
- The hero metric template: big number, small label, repeat three times.
- Phrases: "built for the AI era," "ship faster," "10x your team," and any sentence ending in an em dash.

If a reader cannot tell within five seconds that this page is from a different lineage than the seven sites above, the design has failed.

## Tone rules

- No em dashes anywhere in product copy. Use commas, colons, semicolons, periods, parentheses.
- No exclamation points.
- Numbers earn their place. "Starts in 1.8 seconds" beats "blazing fast."
- One claim per sentence. One idea per paragraph.
- The word "seamless" is banned.

## Hard constraints

- The tagline "Mirror Local. Deploy Cloud. Ship Faster." stays. It is the contractual hook.
- The five product primitives must appear and be nameable: ephemeral pods, mirror sync engine, instant previews, live database sandbox, multiplayer collaboration.
- No login wall. Pricing visible without a contact form.
- Hero copy and primary call to action must work without JavaScript.

## Out of scope for v1

Customer logos, named testimonials, founder portraits. The page can ship without them. We will not fake them. Pricing is in scope per the hard constraint above; the numbers are committed defaults that the company stands behind.
